# Encrypted sync storage

The account Worker accepts opaque encrypted revision descriptors and authenticated ciphertext. It has no private workspace categories, decryption keys, source filenames or plaintext content hashes. Sync routes require a live customer session; mutations additionally require the session CSRF token, exact Origin and active write access. Private responses use `Cache-Control: no-store`. The account service remains separately gated from guest delivery.

## Publication and continuation

`startUpload`, `putUploadChunk` and `finishUpload` reserve and verify immutable ciphertext before `commitRevisions` can publish it. The original upload request, including its metadata reservation, remains immutable. A commit registers revisions separately from its explicit head changes. Expected heads cover exactly the published documents; an optional publication epoch provides bulk replacement protection. Exact successful mutation replay returns the stored receipt before later head or epoch checks. Unchanged head guards do not add head history or advance the epoch.

Publication traverses exact descriptor dependencies, including older archived revisions of documents with newer heads. Parent metadata establishes ancestry without becoming a payload dependency. A durable digest-bound journal processes at most 48 frontier entries per invocation. Each entry validates a ready internal generation and records exact pins; dependency expansion uses a bounded SQL JSON input. Incomplete work returns `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` with `Retry-After: 1`. Retry the identical request to continue its journal. A frontend pause after its bounded transient retries must retain that identity and progress for explicit retry.

The journal states are `validating`, `complete`, `accepted` and `abandoned`. Pending work conservatively blocks new cleanup claims for its account. Final publication atomically checks the live session, exact immutable registration, ready generations, complete journal, head and epoch guards, quota, sequence and receipt. Its bounded state transition activates already recorded pins. Accepted pins have no expiry. Unaccepted expired work can be abandoned only after an atomic receipt check proves no publication. Interrupted or uncertain accepted work never loses retention merely because time elapsed. No separate graph epoch is needed: immutable edges, guarded generation changes and the cleanup barrier preserve the validated graph.

Registration-only commands leave their journal abandoned after acceptance and do not activate retained payload protection. When a command includes archived revisions unrelated to its heads, those validation pins remain separate from the retained head closure. Published protected roots and their dependencies remain retained. Ordinary history reclamation must use the storage lifecycle's explicit eligibility rules; publication does not silently reclaim prior accepted roots.

## Customer storage and recovery

The account workspace reports local save durability independently from remote sync. A failed local save keeps draft recovery visible even when older changes are synchronized. Offline, sign-in, conflict, quota, failed and paused states retain pending encrypted work and lead to retry or the account recovery controls. Local tile edits stay enabled while uploads wait. Suppressing transient tile-saving chrome does not imply durability: the account banner withholds its saved label until the local commit queue drains. Upload scheduling retains the two-second quiet window, deliberate Save flush and visible-only polling.

Fresh account entry opens account access without initializing the local planner. Choosing local use starts its captured repository; later account-settings navigation preserves the existing planner and draft owners. Copy-only reads of older local data use the migration's non-upgrading participant readers and pure planner projection, retaining the complete original branch and older inherited originals without publishing a managed successor. This capture contributes its declared raw originals directly instead of generating another historical snapshot of the derived picker view. Previously stored originals remain intact. The frozen source remains checked through preview and copy, so later local changes require a new preview. Existing interrupted migration and recovery checks still apply.

Opening an existing managed workspace authenticates its current records, current file bodies and inherited-evidence control root before editing. It preserves the archive reference without reading every historical payload. Complete backup and historical comparison authenticate the full local evidence they consume. Synchronization fully authenticates the archive preserved in the exact mapped publication view, rather than reading its local source again after mapping. Damage or missing data in the consumed graph blocks the operation instead of omitting originals. Opening is not an archive-health certificate, and an ordinary edit cannot clear inherited originals or make their cleanup eligible. When an authenticated mapping already binds the incoming archive to the retained local archive, pull preserves that local reference after checking its control record. The incoming archive remains fully authenticated; complete backup still validates the retained local payloads.

Newly captured historical metadata uses the existing maximum token-page target to reduce encrypted records and upload requests within the format's bounds. Stored original pages retain their exact bytes and boundaries. A mapper can reuse its own live, registered archive proof within its preparation cleanup scope; the main profile independently reopens durable ciphertext after the worker returns.

A conflict-free copy into a managed account stages its encrypted managed target and version 2 recovery journal before selecting it. Immediate selection retains the preparation owner and atomically checks the original destination, publication base and every consumed or produced ciphertext observation, then queues an unmapped intent. It does not reopen its own newly produced archive. Resuming after interruption fully reads the saved target under fresh admission before selecting it. Cloud mapping runs after local durability. When there is no published workspace and one pending change, that target establishes it and its exact intent is acknowledged after acceptance. Multiple changes queued before the first publication retain the existing baseline and ordered receipt chain. Existing uncertain commands keep their saved requests and receipt identities. Exact initial own-publication readback completes remote bookkeeping while preserving the current local root and draft owners; the receipt, complete heads, target and archive must match after full remote validation. Restart resumes the saved target without recapturing guest data; an advanced destination blocks activation while retaining the saved copy for download or a fresh preview. Version 1 native copy journals retain their original reader and comparison workflow.

Storage details are explicit observations, refreshed on request. Cloud usage measures the application's logical ciphertext and metadata accounting against account capacity; it does not measure physical provider storage or other devices. The warning begins at 80 percent. Local category counts and content byte subtotals are computed only after decrypting the captured account workspace, and are never sent as server metadata. An unavailable measurement stays unknown. Service recovery health is independently observed; a verified recovery point does not imply that the latest changes are included.

Private backup download and device-local removal use the existing complete workspace and lifecycle operations. Removal requires explicit acknowledgment of backup needs or unsynced loss, then checks the reviewed local revision and open draft owners inside the clearing transaction. A changed revision, switched profile or active draft blocks removal. Cloud account deletion remains a separate action in account settings.

### Explicit unused sync cleanup

`POST /api/v1/sync/cleanup/preview` accepts one exact opaque document/revision reference. Its response includes the internal generation, publication epoch, observed time, estimated removable ciphertext/seed bytes, exact dependencies and blocking reasons. `POST /api/v1/sync/cleanup` requires that same reference, generation and epoch. Both require active customer authority, exact Origin and CSRF. Full quota or paused uploads do not prevent this recovery action. A stale preview must be reviewed again; an interrupted claim retries the same generation.

Only newly created ordinary single-parent publication envelopes can be unprotected. Initial seeds, bulk publication, special resolution paths and all original mapped bodies remain protected. Existing descriptors and ciphertext are immutable, including their protection flag. Cleanup therefore removes unused transport data, not source files or history. The policy keeps the latest 20 distinct published revisions per record and requires an age of at least 30 days. Age uses the server generation's original reservation expiry as a conservative lower bound, never a client timestamp; a revived generation must age again. Local candidate discovery scans bounded cached pages and decrypts each eligible envelope before naming its category. A fresh device without those payloads cannot promise discovery or cleanup.

Every incoming dependency, current head, live scan, active publication work or borrow, and retained service snapshot blocks reclamation. Archived owners still count as dependencies. The final guarded transaction rechecks those exclusions, generation, descriptor, session and epoch; it invalidates affected certificates, retires only the candidate's accepted or abandoned publication pins, and closes the generation atomically. Other pins, original bodies, head history, mutation receipts and descriptors remain. Permanent object fencing and quota accounting then use the existing lifecycle below. A failed marker verification retains liabilities and cannot be treated as successful removal.

An interrupted unaccepted command can retain locally recorded upload readiness after server staging expires. Receipt lookup still runs first. Only a structured remote missing or unfinished response for the exact saved upload permits resetting derived readiness and chunk position. Malformed replies, transport uncertainty, authorization failures or mismatched upload identities retain the original journal without a reset. Revival reuses the original command, upload requests, descriptors and ciphertext, including its continuation allowance.

## Reads and scan retention

An initial scan inserts its lease and captures sequence and publication epoch in one guarded database observation. Indexed first-publication membership and immutable head history reconstruct heads at that watermark, including heads replaced during pagination. Missing historical membership fails with `STALE_CURSOR`. Archived dependencies are resolved by exact revision reads. The signed account-scoped cursor expires after 30 minutes; its lease prevents cleanup during the scan. Expired leases are reclaimed in bounded maintenance work.

Change limits accept integers from 1 through 100 and default to 100. Initial synthetic groups honor the target. Incremental command groups remain indivisible: the first group can exceed a smaller target, up to its 32-unit command bound, and is then returned alone. The hard page maximum is 100 units, counted as the greater of revision and head counts per group. A continuation retains the same watermark and epoch until it has consumed that captured range.

## Accepted physical transitions

`GET /api/v1/sync/mutations/{mutationId}/transition?document={id}&document={id}` returns the physical heads immediately before and after one accepted mutation. Only repeated `document` parameters are accepted, in caller order, with distinct canonical IDs. Limits are `maxMutationTransitionDocuments`, `maxMutationTransitionQueryBytes` (UTF-8 bytes including the question mark) and `maxMutationTransitionResponseBytes` in the shared protocol. The client validates exact account, mutation and ordered coverage, bounds streamed bytes and retains the captured profile/session through parsing and final return. Renewal, switch, lock or caller cancellation discards the result. Cold offline verification remains pending.

The strict `mutation-transition` response binds the accepted request digest, complete receipt and predecessor publication epoch to the authenticated account. The reader selects the latest history strictly before the receipt sequence and at or before that sequence in one guarded D1 observation. Headless commands still have a receipt and sequence, so the previous epoch comes from the exact preceding receipt; it cannot be derived by subtracting from the output epoch or searching only head changes. Later publications do not alter that observation. Read-only accounts retain access, and a final live-authority check rejects revocation during the read. Neither current heads nor caller-supplied sequence/account values choose the historical boundary.

`mutation-transition-unavailable` distinguishes `unknown-mutation` from `history-unavailable`. Missing or malformed expected history, membership, predecessor receipts, stored identities or receipt/output associations produces `history-unavailable`, never partial rows or invented nulls. Native database failures remain `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. A null means no physical publication at that boundary under the complete-history invariant; it does not imply absence of an encrypted seed, tombstone or effective value. An opaque accepted command with parents `[R,Q]` can still have actual previous head N. Encrypted consumers must independently validate allocation, effective selection and role/result association across complete bounded batches. These unsigned online responses are not portable proof authority.

**Lifetime and recovery precondition:** authorize historical facts only for a database created with the initial sync schema and retaining the complete account ledger. `commitRevisions` owns the atomic sequence/receipt/head-history/membership producer. Preserve all account-scoped `mutation_receipts`, `head_history` and `sync_documents.first_sequence` together, including headless receipts, for the account lifetime. Cleanup and expired-work maintenance must not prune this metadata. Generated accounting charges its logical encoded projection, while physical indexes and indefinite growth remain operational liabilities.

Local consistency checks cannot detect every removed intermediate interval, or distinguish jointly removed membership and history from a never-published document. The schema has no intrinsic provenance/completeness marker. Deployment and recovery must prohibit using an uncertified restored, imported or partially migrated ledger as semantic authority. Such a ledger needs a separate completeness-floor/provenance migration before authority is enabled, with an authoritative complete physical baseline, predecessor epoch, coherent account incarnation and contiguous retained transitions. A scalar floor or surviving row count cannot recreate lost truth. Restore must run outside live serving authority or atomically install a fenced complete incarnation and revoke old sessions. Deletion must end read authority before removing proof metadata. The transition fixtures exercise a fresh migrated ledger and detectable corruption; they do not certify a deployed restore or complete account deletion.

Physical metadata retention does not ensure ciphertext availability. Accepted retained roots and actual dependency pins preserve the necessary bodies without an expiry; `protected: true` on an unpublished registration alone does not. Missing ciphertext keeps encrypted proof pending even when physical transition metadata is intact. The read introduces no scan lease, retained pin or persistent quota change; its existing transaction guards are transient.

Run `npm run test:worker -- worker/sync/transitions.test.ts` with `CRYKIT_SYNC_MEASUREMENTS` to reproduce the resource probes. At maximum document cardinality, local Miniflare D1 measured seven direct-reader statements across a five-statement observation and a two-statement final authority check, with approximately 270 native rows read and six transient guard writes. Reads at approximately 100, 300 and 800 retained history rows had the same measured row cost. Query plans used the account/mutation and account/document/sequence indexes; the bounded requested-order sort remains. Maximum SQL was approximately 1.5 KB, bindings stayed below ten, and successful route payloads were approximately 6.3 KB. Unique maximum-width protocol values also fit the 8 KiB response budget. Complete control, fixed-directory and selected-document physical coverage required three bounded calls in the fixture.

Those native metadata measurements cover successful direct-reader batches. A revoked-authority probe added a classification query; failed batches and standalone classification reads are not included in native row totals. Customer middleware adds four statements by source inspection, not by that direct-reader measurement. Local elapsed times are recorded in the measurement output, not treated as hosted CPU or latency bounds. This read does not change the full sync protocol's Paid deployment requirement or establish Free compatibility. Provider enforcement, physical database growth and recovery certification remain deployment responsibilities.

## Permanent object fences

Every internal generation uses fresh opaque object keys. Before any external write, D1 assigns and charges each chunk's ciphertext obligation and its fixed 32-byte seed and 32-byte removal marker. Only one initializer right exists; it uses create-if-absent. A lost initializer response permits verification of the assigned seed but never another initializer attempt on that key. A guarded second transition records the original seed ETag and authorizes an immutable payload write conditional on that ETag. Returned ETags do not establish content integrity: length and SHA-256 are independently verified.

Cleanup claims an eligible generation before writing its assigned content-free marker. It verifies exact marker bytes and an ETag different from the authorized seed predicate before releasing ciphertext and seed charges. A marker equality or verification failure leaves full liabilities and a visible `unsafe` state; ordinary retries cannot clear it. Managed keys are never deleted, expired or reused. Late initializers fail create-if-absent, late payloads fail their original predicate, and identical late marker writes preserve the same fence. Revival preserves the public upload and revision identities while allocating a fresh internal generation.

The fence relies on R2 conditional replacement and strong consistency as documented in the [Workers API reference](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/workers/workers-api-reference/), checked on 2026-10-11. Local tests gate actual conditional writes across marker installation; hosted overlapping writes remain a deployment verification requirement.

Permanent marker bodies and minimal opaque reconciliation metadata remain globally charged. `prepareClosedLiabilityTransfer` returns a guard and statements for the deletion owner to compose with independent ledger acknowledgment and its account-removal transaction. It cannot establish that acknowledgment itself. The removal owner must preserve those global liabilities across account deletion and recovery. Content removal does not mean that every opaque service control object has disappeared.

## Accounting and maintenance interfaces

`quota_counters` tracks ciphertext/control reservations, descriptor reservations and exact encoded metadata row, byte and dependency-edge totals. Each owned table has a generated logical JSON-tuple byte count and transactional counter triggers. These counts describe the defined encoded source projection, not physical SQLite pages or indexes. Counter rows themselves and non-sync authoritative tables belong in the complete recovery projection and its separate capture-eligibility accounting. A final quota check runs in the same guarded transaction as metadata and reservation changes.

The shared service interfaces are `generationUnpinnedSql`, `generationCleanupStatements`, `claimGenerationCleanup`, `fenceGeneration`, `cleanupExpiredUploads`, `reconcileExpiredPublicationWork` and `prepareClosedLiabilityTransfer`. Snapshot jobs must establish their exact generation pins or conservative barrier before claiming recoverable bytes; closed or unsafe generations cannot satisfy readiness. Account deletion needs its own guarded authority to close retained generations, plus independent revocation-ledger acknowledgment. Ordinary user cleanup must prove eligibility before removing accepted retention. None of these owners may introduce unconditional payload copies or managed-key deletion.

Scheduled maintenance reconciles expired unpublished journals and scan leases, then handles one eligible generation and at most 32 chunk fences. Large generations resume across calls. The 24-hour reservation lifetime makes unretained staging eligible; it does not promise a finite physical deletion deadline. Unsafe fences remain charged and require separate investigation.

## Deployment limits and reproducible measurements

Cloudflare documentation checked on 2026-10-11 lists 50 D1 queries per Worker invocation on Free and 1,000 on Paid, a 2,000,000-byte row/string limit, 100 bound parameters, 100,000-byte SQL statements and a 30-second batch limit. Free databases are limited to 500 MB. See [D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/). Supported maximum sync operations can exceed the Free invocation limit, so the admitted protocol requires Paid deployment unless a separately verified configuration supplies equivalent bounded transactions. Smaller client batches reduce work but do not establish Free compatibility for the full protocol.

Run `npm run test:worker -- worker/sync/uploads.test.ts worker/sync/commit.test.ts` to reproduce the guarded D1/R2 checks. Set `CRYKIT_SYNC_MEASUREMENTS` to a writable private JSONL output file to retain row, byte, statement, binding, invocation and timing measurements. The scenarios include a transitive graph larger than the direct dependency bound, maximum revision/head commands, dense dependency metadata, and all bounded chunk slots. Invocation measurements directly count sync queries; the documented customer middleware adds its SELECT and guarded session touch. Hosted enforcement, Worker CPU/memory, database page/index growth and overlapping R2 ordering require isolated deployment measurements before launch.

The local maximum-input fixtures observed approximately 760 sync statements in the most expensive dense-metadata call, plus four middleware statements established by source inspection. That command carries the maximum direct dependency list on every revision and remains below the 1 MiB commit cap. Dependency validation, journal expansion and edge registration use set-based JSON queries, so extra edges increase row work and encoded bytes without one query per edge. Maximum chunk slots and two-parent commands are measured separately because all metadata dimensions share that commit cap. The largest observed encoded row was approximately 730 KB, with approximately 98,000 rows read and 48,000 written in the final guarded batch. These local measurements justify a 48-entry frontier budget with room for finalization and failure reconciliation; they do not establish hosted CPU or latency limits.

The complete additive-bulk conflict fixture observed approximately 13 million rows read and 310,000 written across approximately 12,000 successful sync transaction events, taking about three minutes locally. This is the whole journey: real service authentication, independent devices, a first-allocation race against additions crossing `maxChangedHeads`, an unrelated same-shard edit, complete comparison, explicit resolution and independent adoption. Its shared Editor original is only a UTF-16 code unit; these totals cannot be attributed to a large binary upload. Run `CRYKIT_SYNC_PROOF_PHASES=<private-jsonl-path> npx vitest run --project persistence src/persistence/mapping-bulk-conflicts.test.ts --maxWorkers=1` after preparing the account test bundles. The receipt sums the returned D1 result metadata for successful sync transactions; it excludes outer guard insert/delete statements, direct authentication/read queries and failed batches. Trigger/index work is whatever the local D1 implementation reports. These are incomplete local workload observations, not per-save costs, complete billing totals or physical IO measurements.

[D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/), checked on 2026-10-16, specifies account-wide Workers Free allowances of 5 million rows read and 100,000 written per day, resetting at midnight UTC. The locally observed bulk fixture exceeds both daily allowances; hosted exhaustion has not been tested. Free queries fail after a daily allowance is exhausted, while stored data remains intact, as documented in [Free-tier enforcement](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-09-01-d1-free-tier-limit-enforcement/). Workers Paid includes D1 usage with monthly allowances and usage-based overages. The full protocol's Paid requirement therefore also has a workload-dependent daily-volume justification. Smaller workloads and paused cloud work can preserve local editing, but are not a verified Free-compatible configuration for the full protocol. Hosted measurements and account-wide usage monitoring are still required; a successful Paid deployment does not establish a predictable cost or efficient bulk traversal.

For closed keys, the permanent body estimate is `closed_chunk_keys * 32` bytes, plus retained encoded metadata and physical database/index overhead. Cleanup needs at least one marker PUT and one verification GET per key, plus its guarded accounting work; interruptions add retries. Deletion is therefore not net-zero service growth. Monitor retained marker count/bytes, unsafe fences, metadata growth and global headroom. Successful local or Paid tests do not prove Free support, a daily backup schedule or a particular recovery-retention window.
