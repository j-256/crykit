# Encrypted sync storage

The account Worker accepts opaque encrypted revision descriptors and authenticated ciphertext. It has no private workspace categories, decryption keys, source filenames or plaintext content hashes. Sync routes require a live customer session; mutations additionally require the session CSRF token, exact Origin and active write access. Private responses use `Cache-Control: no-store`. The account service remains separately gated from guest delivery.

## Publication and continuation

`startUpload`, `putUploadChunk` and `finishUpload` reserve and verify immutable ciphertext before `commitRevisions` can publish it. The original upload request, including its metadata reservation, remains immutable. A commit registers revisions separately from its explicit head changes. Expected heads cover exactly the published documents; an optional publication epoch provides bulk replacement protection. Exact successful mutation replay returns the stored receipt before later head or epoch checks. Unchanged head guards do not add head history or advance the epoch.

Publication traverses exact descriptor dependencies, including older archived revisions of documents with newer heads. Parent metadata establishes ancestry without becoming a payload dependency. A durable digest-bound journal processes at most 48 frontier entries per invocation. Each entry validates a ready internal generation and records exact pins; dependency expansion uses a bounded SQL JSON input. Incomplete work returns `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` with `Retry-After: 1`. Retry the identical request to continue its journal. A frontend pause after its bounded transient retries must retain that identity and progress for explicit retry.

The journal states are `validating`, `complete`, `accepted` and `abandoned`. Pending work conservatively blocks new cleanup claims for its account. Final publication atomically checks the live session, exact immutable registration, ready generations, complete journal, head and epoch guards, quota, sequence and receipt. Its bounded state transition activates already recorded pins. Accepted pins have no expiry. Unaccepted expired work can be abandoned only after an atomic receipt check proves no publication. Interrupted or uncertain accepted work never loses retention merely because time elapsed. No separate graph epoch is needed: immutable edges, guarded generation changes and the cleanup barrier preserve the validated graph.

Registration-only commands leave their journal abandoned after acceptance and do not activate retained payload protection. When a command includes archived revisions unrelated to its heads, those validation pins remain separate from the retained head closure. Published protected roots and their dependencies remain retained. Ordinary history reclamation must use the storage lifecycle's explicit eligibility rules; publication does not silently reclaim prior accepted roots.

## Customer storage and recovery

The account workspace reports local save durability independently from remote sync. A failed local save keeps draft recovery visible even when older changes are synchronized. Offline, sign-in, conflict, quota, failed and paused states retain pending encrypted work and lead to retry or the account recovery controls. Local tile edits stay enabled while uploads wait. Suppressing transient tile-saving chrome does not imply durability: the account banner withholds its saved label until the local commit queue drains. Upload scheduling retains the two-second quiet window, deliberate Save flush and visible-only polling.

Fresh account entry opens account access without initializing the local planner. New accounts initialize the supported launch generation directly with complete vanilla and guide snapshots; no per-account reset or generation transition is needed. Choosing local use starts its captured repository. Explicit local copy reads a supported launch workspace, discloses the selected records and inherited originals, and retains the frozen source through preview and commit. Later source changes require a new preview. Prelaunch browser data is not automatically migrated or uploaded.

Opening an existing managed workspace authenticates its current records, current file bodies and inherited-evidence control root before editing. It preserves the archive reference without reading every historical payload. Complete backup and historical comparison authenticate the full local evidence they consume. Synchronization fully authenticates the archive preserved in the exact mapped publication view, rather than reading its local source again after mapping. Damage or missing data in the consumed graph blocks the operation instead of omitting originals. Opening is not an archive-health certificate, and an ordinary edit cannot clear inherited originals or make their cleanup eligible. When an authenticated mapping already binds the incoming archive to the retained local archive, pull preserves that local reference after checking its control record. The incoming archive remains fully authenticated; complete backup still validates the retained local payloads.

Newly captured historical metadata uses the existing maximum token-page target to reduce encrypted records and upload requests within the format's bounds. Stored original pages retain their exact bytes and boundaries. A mapper can reuse its own live, registered archive proof within its preparation cleanup scope; the main profile independently reopens durable ciphertext after the worker returns.

A conflict-free copy into a managed account stages its encrypted managed target and version 2 recovery journal before selecting it. Immediate selection retains the preparation owner and atomically checks the original destination, publication base and every consumed or produced ciphertext observation, then queues an unmapped intent. It does not reopen its own newly produced archive. Resuming after interruption fully reads the saved target under fresh admission before selecting it. Cloud mapping runs after local durability. When there is no published workspace and one pending change, that target establishes it and its exact intent is acknowledged after acceptance. Multiple changes queued before the first publication retain the existing baseline and ordered receipt chain. Existing uncertain commands keep their saved requests and receipt identities. Exact initial own-publication readback completes remote bookkeeping while preserving the current local root and draft owners; the receipt, complete heads, target and archive must match after full remote validation. Restart resumes the saved target without recapturing guest data; an advanced destination blocks activation while retaining the saved copy for download or a fresh preview. Version 1 native copy journals retain their original reader and comparison workflow.

Storage details are explicit observations, refreshed on request. Cloud usage measures the application's logical ciphertext and metadata accounting against account capacity; it does not measure physical provider storage or other devices. The warning begins at 80 percent. Local category counts and content byte subtotals are computed only after decrypting the captured account workspace, and are never sent as server metadata. An unavailable measurement stays unknown. Service recovery health is independently observed; a verified recovery point does not imply that the latest changes are included.

Private backup download and device-local removal use the existing complete workspace and lifecycle operations. Removal requires explicit acknowledgment of backup needs or unsynced loss, then checks the reviewed local revision and open draft owners inside the clearing transaction. A changed revision, switched profile or active draft blocks removal. Cloud account deletion remains a separate action in account settings.

### Sign-in source decisions

Automatic sign-in reconciliation captures only existing guest storage after authentication establishes the new profile epoch. An encrypted, versioned decision binds the account, exact guest instance/revision vector, validated content digest, chosen action, and saved operation identity. Only the outer capture ID and time are excluded from the source digest. Changed or restored sources and different accounts do not inherit approval; unreadable sources remain unavailable rather than becoming empty. Prompt summaries and Playthrough labels remain in memory and never enter diagnostic records.

Fresh account preparation validates remote authority before offering destination-sensitive choices. A new account publishes its own initialized baseline first, without guest data. Previously admitted account use and exact completed decisions can reopen offline; a newly initialized empty cache alone is not admission. Preparation and opening transfer scheduler ownership only after the actual prior runner settles, including retired profiles. Server retry deadlines survive same-profile owner replacement. Recovery listing runs on startup and authenticated reconnection without blocking admitted offline opening.

Merge keeps its exact saved copy identity across retries. The source decision, selected native or managed target, and intent activate atomically; a completed replay verifies the original attached decision and activation proof rather than writing a missing receipt afterward. Retained preparations use fresh remote discovery without ordinary sync rewriting their saved publication base. Account-only choice records a receipt against the unchanged local head and leaves guest storage untouched. Device replacement uses the guarded reviewed replacement and complete recovery flow below. UI selection follows durable activation as a retryable provider effect, so selection failure cannot trigger another import.

Cancellation before activation returns through provider local selection. Failed remote cleanup remains explicitly pending in the encrypted decision; cancellation after activation does not imply rollback. A paused replacement has separate renewed-review, private-backup, decline, and Use latest actions. Decline opens the retained branch with its paused authority intact; only confirmed Use latest retires it through the sealed branch-selection transaction.

### Encrypted account replacement recovery

Recovery snapshots are dedicated, self-contained encrypted revision sets, separate from sync revisions and permanent publication history. The server stores only ciphertext descriptors and opaque operation, upload, revision, and publication identities. Descriptors cannot have parents or dependencies. An upload assigned to recovery cannot later enter ordinary sync history, including after recovery retirement; the permanent upload ownership marker remains quota charged.

`POST /api/v1/sync/recoveries` reserves an operation's exact descriptor set and declared ciphertext total. Identical retries reuse that point; changed input is rejected. The account admits at most three points, including pending preparations, within the smaller of 128 MiB or account capacity. Admission includes ciphertext, descriptors, and bounded control overhead; account quota additionally charges actual logical metadata, transport controls, and reserved bytes. The oldest completed point can retire to admit a replacement. Sealed pending or unresolved work cannot be evicted. An oversized or full reservation never authorizes replacement.

A new point is pending and unsealed. Its byte reservation transfers into normal upload liabilities as `uploads`, chunk writes, and upload completion proceed. Incomplete transport keeps ordinary expiry and same-identity revival; it is not pinned indefinitely. Fenced unsealed chunks transfer displaced ciphertext and seed charges back into their preparation reservation, while old markers stay charged and revival separately admits new marker overhead. `POST /recoveries/{operationId}/seal` captures only complete ready generations and pins their ciphertext. The original complete source and exact encrypted upload bytes must remain locally recoverable until sealing succeeds.

The client recovery service requires an owned complete workspace snapshot. It holds that capture's freshness fence while producing a self-contained encrypted backup, counts the bounded archive before revision encryption, and authenticates the produced archive through the complete-backup reader before upload. Planner data, Progress, editor drafts, saves, assets, file history, conflicts, and inherited evidence travel together. An encrypted versioned journal and the exact archive descriptor are installed atomically before reservation. Concurrent callers adopt that journal's upload and ciphertext identity. Failed reservation or transfer keeps those exact bytes available for retry; it never installs recovery-only evidence in the account's permanent graph.

Each device separately bounds retained retry archives by the recovery point limit and aggregate recovery byte limit. Opaque live-archive pointers participate in atomic local admission; an early bound avoids preparing a candidate that cannot fit, and the installation transaction repeats admission against concurrent callers. Failed local admission releases only that caller's private candidate. Sealed and terminal journals retain minimal encrypted metadata but no archive payload. This transport bound does not substitute for account-wide server quota or point admission. It covers installed, resumable archives, not all temporary browser bytes: preparation ownership is in memory before atomic installation, so abrupt process termination can leave uninstalled orphan chunks in the shared staging infrastructure. Normal exceptions and cancellation release private candidates; pre-install crash cleanup requires a separate shared-staging ownership solution.

`GET /recoveries/{operationId}/status` reports `unknown`, `active`, or `retired` from existing account-scoped point, cancellation, supersession, and upload ownership. Listing and startup reconciliation are read-only remotely: they remove dedicated local archive ciphertext only after exact sealed-point readback or authenticated permanent terminal ownership. An absent listing or unknown operation never authorizes deletion or a new reservation. These reads remain available when capacity is below retained usage. Offline cleanup waits for authenticated reconnection; an expired remote point leaves no permanent local archive once that reconciliation succeeds. Capturing a sealed recovery downloads and authenticates its bytes into temporary encrypted backup staging, whose caller must dispose after use.

Reviewed workspace replacement uses a separate versioned encrypted authority alongside the unchanged mapped-intent and publication journals. It binds the selected source operation to the reviewed publication epoch and complete head set. Sealing the displaced snapshot precedes local selection; the selected head, mapped intent, authority, and optional encrypted sign-in decision receipt commit in one fenced IndexedDB transaction. Native and managed selections retain their normal source, dependency, admission, and profile-abort checks. Receipt-only decisions use an exact-head transaction without creating a mapped intent or changing the local revision.

A preparation journal binds the operation ID, selected-source revision vector and validated metadata digest, local fence, reviewed remote heads and epoch, and predecessor recovery. Source identity omits only the outer snapshot capture ID and time; nested original identities, times, and content remain part of the digest. Reopening the same operation with an unchanged supported source capture resumes its installed recovery ciphertext rather than regenerating snapshot identity or allocating another point. Before local activation, target staging remains a live capability: callers reopen the same validated source and repeat preparation against the unchanged review. A changed source or branch requires a new review; a successful atomic activation records that preparation as activated. Completed sign-in decisions verify their own activated preparation, the authenticated target dependency shared with the decision receipt, and the migration journal at the exact next fence revision. The stage and decision operation IDs are independently allocated; a later replacement can become the active authority without invalidating that historical proof. Missing or altered associations block reopening. Callers release stages, reviews, snapshots, and receipt attachments after use. Uninstalled ciphertext retains the shared staging crash limitation described above.

Sync checks the exact original final receipt before reconsidering remote movement. If a completion response is lost and the completed recovery payload is then retired, replay requires the encrypted acknowledged binding, terminal operation status, and an authenticated accepted mutation transition whose full request digest matches that binding and the saved final publication. Retirement alone also covers cancellation and supersession, so it never establishes acceptance. Exact accepted replay releases the pending publication without reserving, uploading, or extending retention again. The final commit and its authority become durable together before binding; a successor must acknowledge its binding and linked supersession before any submission. A definite head rejection pauses the complete local branch and its later edits. Retry and foreground polling do not renew replacement approval. A changed reviewed epoch or head set also pauses before pull selection or publication preparation. Explicit renewed approval captures the complete local target and preserves the original plus newly displaced account sources in a sealed successor. The original recovery remains protected until the exact linked handoff succeeds.

Selecting latest account data first seals the complete paused branch, including later edits and inherited originals. The selection transaction compares the whole pending-intent set, rejected publication, old authority, and local fence, archives their encrypted proof, retires them together, and selects a fresh fully validated remote projection. Uncertain publication, failed preservation, stale review, or stale local state leaves the branch active. Only this sealed, guarded branch-selection capability can discard managed historical originals from the active projection; ordinary pulls retain their existing restriction. The read-only switch has no publication receipt, so its recovery remains pending and protected for a future verified reconciliation. Structured client diagnostics expose only phase, opaque operation identity, duration, outcome, and safe error code.

Before replacement publication, `POST /recoveries/{operationId}/bind` records the exact guarded commit, including its mutation ID, expected publication epoch, expected heads, target heads, and request digest. The commit carries `recoveryOperationId`; publication rejects missing or mismatched association, including omission on an already-bound mutation. Binding requires a sealed pending point. A new binding can supersede an old one only with explicit `supersedesMutationId`, no accepted old receipt, and a server publication epoch newer than the old expectation. An unknown receipt at the unchanged epoch remains uncertain. Acceptance time is written atomically with the accepted mutation receipt.

Rebinding preserves the same immutable snapshot and is suitable only when its displaced content remains complete. When renewed approval changes the remote state to be displaced, the client prepares a new operation containing the original saved sources plus that newly displaced state. After independently validating that complete encrypted backup, sealing it, and binding its new guarded publication, the client calls `POST /recoveries/{operationId}/supersede` with both operation and mutation identities. The version 1 atomic guard requires a sealed bound successor, a newer successor expectation, a superseded old epoch, and no accepted old receipt. It retires old ownership and retains a quota-charged terminal successor link for exact retries. The server proves those control conditions; it cannot inspect or certify encrypted content coverage. Three independent unresolved points still block another preparation.

A replacement can seal its recovery before a final publication mutation exists. If renewed review displaces newer account data during that interval, supersession version 2 identifies the predecessor as `sealed-unbound` rather than inventing a mutation. Its atomic guard requires the old point to remain sealed with no mutation, binding, or acceptance, and a sealed bound successor with the complete saved sources. A concurrent old binding prevents this handoff; a successful handoff leaves the same permanent terminal link that prevents delayed binding or reservation from reviving the old point. Bound predecessors continue to use the unchanged version 1 request and advanced-epoch proof. Both variants return the version 1 link receipt because the acknowledged successor relationship has the same meaning.

Successor preparation independently reads back its encrypted archive and compares both newly displaced current data and every original metadata stream and body with the captured sources. Its encrypted coverage receipt binds the exact predecessor operation, descriptors, and metadata digest to the exact successor descriptor. Coverage version 1 additionally binds the exact old mutation; version 2 records the distinct sealed-unbound state. Neither variant can substitute the other after preparation. Supersession additionally requires the locally persisted acknowledgment of the exact final binding, not merely a request to bind. If a binding response is lost, the coordinator retries its saved full commit through `bind` before calling `supersede`; the server checks the original request digest before the client records acknowledgment. Changed or accepted predecessor bindings remain protected.

`POST /recoveries/{operationId}/complete` verifies that bound receipt and records completion and expiry from the stored acceptance time. Retention lasts 30 days from successful publication acceptance, so a delayed completion request cannot extend it. `GET /recoveries` returns descriptors, pending/completed state, sealing status, bound mutation ID, acceptance time, and retention dates. A fresh authenticated device can complete an accepted pending point using its operation ID without recovering the original client journal. A sealed point with no accepted publication remains protected until that publication is explicitly reconciled. Dedicated `GET /recoveries/{operationId}/revisions/{revisionId}/chunks/{index}` reads authenticate account and point ownership before returning verified ciphertext.

`POST /recoveries/{operationId}/cancel` is only for explicit cancellation of unsealed, unbound preparation. It cannot cancel a sealed point or an accepted/bound replacement. Cancellation atomically competes with sealing, frees the point and unconsumed byte reservation, and retains an opaque terminal marker charged against account quota. A never-reserved operation receives only this marker, guarded against a concurrent reserve; abandoning it does not require a free point slot or archive capacity. New markers need ordinary metadata quota, while existing terminal replay and existing-point cleanup remain available below retained usage. Terminal markers are not age-pruned because forgetting them would permit late retries to resurrect cancelled work. Started transport remains quota charged until ordinary fenced cleanup completes.

`POST /recoveries/{operationId}/remove`, bounded expiry maintenance, and admission eviction retire only completed points. Logical retirement removes listing/read authority and transfers payload cleanup to permanent upload ownership. Independent service snapshot, publication, and scan pins still prevent physical fencing; retained ciphertext stays fully quota charged even after its recovery slot is freed. A `pending` removal result means payload cleanup remains pending, not that replacement has gained recovery authority. Fencing releases payload charges only after verified content-free markers; unsafe or interrupted cleanup keeps its liabilities. Service snapshots include recovery descriptors, binding/acceptance records, cancellation and supersession markers, and upload ownership; restore remaps sealed generation IDs while preserving exact legacy upload tuples. Account deletion removes recovery ownership through its existing independent revocation and fenced-object lifecycle.

All relative routes above live under `/api/v1/sync`. Writes require the normal exact customer session, Origin, CSRF, service admission, and bounded request checks. The shared client binds private response parsing to the captured profile/session and validates operation/account association. These storage APIs do not activate a replacement or decrypt or interpret the workspace backup.

### Explicit unused sync cleanup

`POST /api/v1/sync/cleanup/preview` accepts one exact opaque document/revision reference. Its response includes the internal generation, publication epoch, observed time, estimated removable ciphertext/seed bytes, exact dependencies and blocking reasons. `POST /api/v1/sync/cleanup` requires that same reference, generation and epoch. Both require active customer authority, exact Origin and CSRF. Full quota or paused uploads do not prevent this recovery action. A stale preview must be reviewed again; an interrupted claim retries the same generation.

Only newly created ordinary single-parent publication envelopes can be unprotected. Initial seeds, bulk publication, special resolution paths and all original mapped bodies remain protected. Existing descriptors and ciphertext are immutable, including their protection flag. Cleanup therefore removes unused transport data, not source files or history. The policy keeps the latest 20 distinct published revisions per record and requires an age of at least 30 days. Age uses the server generation's original reservation expiry as a conservative lower bound, never a client timestamp; a revived generation must age again. Local candidate discovery scans bounded cached pages and decrypts each eligible envelope before naming its category. A fresh device without those payloads cannot promise discovery or cleanup.

Every incoming dependency, current head, live scan, active publication work or borrow, and retained service snapshot blocks reclamation. Archived owners still count as dependencies. The final guarded transaction rechecks those exclusions, generation, descriptor, session and epoch; it invalidates affected certificates, retires only the candidate's accepted or abandoned publication pins, and closes the generation atomically. Other pins, original bodies, head history, mutation receipts and descriptors remain. Permanent object fencing and quota accounting then use the existing lifecycle below. A failed marker verification retains liabilities and cannot be treated as successful removal.

An interrupted unaccepted command can retain locally recorded upload readiness after server staging expires. Receipt lookup still runs first. Only a structured remote missing or unfinished response for the exact saved upload permits resetting derived readiness and chunk position. Malformed replies, transport uncertainty, authorization failures or mismatched upload identities retain the original journal without a reset. Revival reuses the original command, upload requests, descriptors and ciphertext, including its continuation allowance.

## Reads and scan retention

An initial scan inserts its lease and captures sequence and publication epoch in one guarded database observation. Indexed first-publication membership and immutable head history reconstruct heads at that watermark, including heads replaced during pagination. Missing historical membership fails with `STALE_CURSOR`. Archived dependencies are resolved by exact revision reads. The signed account-scoped cursor expires after 30 minutes; its lease prevents cleanup during the scan. Expired leases are reclaimed in bounded maintenance work.

Change limits accept integers from 1 through 100 and default to 100. Initial synthetic groups honor the target. Incremental command groups remain indivisible: the first group can exceed a smaller target, up to its 32-unit command bound, and is then returned alone. The hard page maximum is 100 units, counted as the greater of revision and head counts per group. A continuation retains the same watermark and epoch until it has consumed that captured range.

## Accepted physical transitions

`GET /api/v1/sync/mutations/{mutationId}/transition?document={id}&document={id}` returns the physical heads immediately before and after one accepted mutation. Only repeated `document` parameters are accepted, in caller order, with distinct canonical IDs. Limits are `maxMutationTransitionDocuments`, `maxMutationTransitionQueryBytes` (UTF-8 bytes including the question mark) and `maxMutationTransitionResponseBytes` in the shared protocol. The client validates exact account, mutation and ordered coverage, bounds streamed bytes and retains the captured profile/session through parsing and final return. Renewal, switch, lock or caller cancellation discards the result. Cold offline verification remains pending.

The strict `mutation-transition` response binds the accepted request digest, complete receipt and predecessor publication epoch to the authenticated account. The reader selects the latest history strictly before the receipt sequence and at or before that sequence in one guarded D1 observation. Headless commands still have a receipt and sequence, so the previous epoch comes from the exact preceding receipt; it cannot be derived by subtracting from the output epoch or searching only head changes. Later publications do not alter that observation. Read-only accounts retain access, and a final live-authority check rejects revocation during the read. Neither current heads nor caller-supplied sequence/account values choose the historical boundary.

`mutation-transition-unavailable` distinguishes `unknown-mutation` from `history-unavailable`. Missing or malformed expected history, membership, predecessor receipts, stored identities or receipt/output associations produces `history-unavailable`, never partial rows or invented nulls. Native database failures remain `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`. A null means no physical publication at that boundary under the complete-history invariant; it does not imply absence of an encrypted seed, tombstone or effective value. An opaque accepted command with parents `[R,Q]` can still have actual previous head N. Encrypted consumers must independently validate allocation, effective selection and role/result association across complete bounded batches. These unsigned online responses are not portable proof authority.

**Lifetime and recovery precondition:** authorize historical facts only for a database created with the initial sync schema and retaining the complete account ledger. `commitRevisions` owns the atomic sequence/receipt/head-history/membership producer. Preserve all account-scoped `mutation_receipts`, `head_history` and `sync_documents.first_sequence` together, including headless receipts, for the account lifetime. Cleanup and expired-work maintenance must not prune this metadata. Generated accounting charges its logical encoded projection, while physical indexes and indefinite growth remain operational liabilities.

Local consistency checks cannot detect every removed intermediate interval, or distinguish jointly removed membership and history from a never-published document. The schema has no intrinsic provenance/completeness marker. Deployment and recovery must prohibit using an uncertified restored, imported or partially migrated ledger as semantic authority. Such a ledger needs a separate completeness-floor/provenance migration before authority is enabled, with an authoritative complete physical baseline, predecessor epoch, coherent account incarnation and contiguous retained transitions. A scalar floor or surviving row count cannot recreate lost truth. Restore must run outside live serving authority or atomically install a fenced complete incarnation and revoke old sessions. Deletion must end read authority before removing proof metadata. The transition fixtures exercise a fresh migrated ledger and detectable corruption; they do not certify a deployed restore or complete account deletion.

Physical metadata retention does not ensure ciphertext availability. Accepted retained roots and actual dependency pins preserve the necessary bodies without an expiry; `protected: true` on an unpublished registration alone does not. Missing ciphertext keeps encrypted proof pending even when physical transition metadata is intact. The read introduces no scan lease, retained pin or persistent quota change; its existing transaction guards are transient.

## Permanent object fences

Every internal generation uses fresh opaque object keys. Before any external write, D1 assigns and charges each chunk's ciphertext obligation and its fixed 32-byte seed and 32-byte removal marker. Only one initializer right exists; it uses create-if-absent. A lost initializer response permits verification of the assigned seed but never another initializer attempt on that key. A guarded second transition records the original seed ETag and authorizes an immutable payload write conditional on that ETag. Returned ETags do not establish content integrity: length and SHA-256 are independently verified.

Cleanup claims an eligible generation before writing its assigned content-free marker. It verifies exact marker bytes and an ETag different from the authorized seed predicate before releasing ciphertext and seed charges. A marker equality or verification failure leaves full liabilities and a visible `unsafe` state; ordinary retries cannot clear it. Managed keys are never deleted, expired or reused. Late initializers fail create-if-absent, late payloads fail their original predicate, and identical late marker writes preserve the same fence. Revival preserves the public upload and revision identities while allocating a fresh internal generation.

The fence relies on R2 conditional replacement and strong consistency as documented in the [Workers API reference](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/workers/workers-api-reference/), checked on 2026-10-11. Local tests gate actual conditional writes across marker installation; hosted overlapping writes remain a deployment verification requirement.

Permanent marker bodies and minimal opaque reconciliation metadata remain globally charged. `prepareClosedLiabilityTransfer` returns a guard and statements for the deletion owner to compose with independent ledger acknowledgment and its account-removal transaction. It cannot establish that acknowledgment itself. The removal owner must preserve those global liabilities across account deletion and recovery. Content removal does not mean that every opaque service control object has disappeared.

## Accounting and maintenance interfaces

`quota_counters` tracks ciphertext/control reservations, descriptor reservations and exact encoded metadata row, byte and dependency-edge totals. Each owned table has a generated logical JSON-tuple byte count and transactional counter triggers. These counts describe the defined encoded source projection, not physical SQLite pages or indexes. Counter rows themselves and non-sync authoritative tables belong in the complete recovery projection and its separate capture-eligibility accounting. A final quota check runs in the same guarded transaction as metadata and reservation changes.

The shared service interfaces are `generationUnpinnedSql`, `generationCleanupStatements`, `claimGenerationCleanup`, `fenceGeneration`, `cleanupExpiredUploads`, `reconcileExpiredPublicationWork` and `prepareClosedLiabilityTransfer`. Snapshot jobs must establish their exact generation pins or conservative barrier before claiming recoverable bytes; closed or unsafe generations cannot satisfy readiness. Account deletion needs its own guarded authority to close retained generations, plus independent revocation-ledger acknowledgment. Ordinary user cleanup must prove eligibility before removing accepted retention. None of these owners may introduce unconditional payload copies or managed-key deletion.

Scheduled maintenance reconciles expired unpublished journals and scan leases, then handles one eligible generation and at most 32 chunk fences. Large generations resume across calls. The 24-hour reservation lifetime makes unretained staging eligible; it does not promise a finite physical deletion deadline. Unsafe fences remain charged and require separate investigation.

### Complete source snapshots

Source bodies are self-contained in both guest and encrypted account graphs. `compressed-catalog-snapshot` documents use `deflate-json-pages-v1`, with bounded canonical pages and a checksum over the complete normalized source. The decoder rejects missing pages, excess expansion, altered payloads, and package-only pins. Deduplication can reuse identical immutable records but cannot replace a historical body with a dependency on the installed application.

### Account entry and recovery controls

Successful sign-in and local unlocking continue automatically into account preparation. Before authentication retires the local profile, open drafts, file operations, and queued immediate Progress saves must finish or be saved or discarded. Security output from recovery or device approval remains on screen until acknowledged. Reconnect, session renewal, and ordinary account settings keep their existing navigation behavior.

Meaningful device work opens Choose what to keep with separate device and account summaries. Merge is recommended, but no replacement choice is selected by default. Merge includes Builds, Progress grouped by exact Playthrough identity, and other meaningful records with their required companions; equivalent records deduplicate and conflicting saved versions remain available for review. Account data and device data each require a separate confirmation. Account-only opening leaves the original device workspace unchanged. Device replacement affects other signed-in devices only through the reviewed publication protocol and preserves displaced account data first. The original guest workspace remains accessible through Continue using this device.

The exact Merge target, pending-copy pointer, and preparing decision are installed together before presenting confirmation. Reload resumes that preparation without recapturing its source or changing its operation identity. This preparation selects no new head, queues no publication intent, and uploads no guest data. A later confirmation atomically activates the target and completed receipt. Retry opening follows that durable result.

Account settings expose encrypted recovery points for private download or a fresh reconciliation. A recovery entry uses a receipt scoped to the selected point, separate from ordinary sign-in. A completed prior recovery choice never skips a new explicit review, including empty or starter-only snapshots; an interrupted preparation resumes its exact saved copy instead. Selected-account startup and local unlocking discover the encrypted preparing recovery receipt before ordinary sync can run; disconnected access keeps that review owner visible until explicit reconnect or cancellation. Cancelling recovery review returns to the selected account. Recovery captures remain owned until all reads finish, then release their staging resources.

Recovery capacity is bounded by the point and byte limits above; completed points retain their server-confirmed acceptance deadline, while pending replacements remain protected. Quota or failed storage blocks replacement instead of dropping recovery work. Offline use can open an already admitted account copy, but fresh merges, replacements, and recovery downloads need authenticated remote data. A paused replacement remains visibly paused after opening; Account sync and Account settings provide review, private backup, keep-working, and confirmed Use latest account data actions. Private backups are plaintext exports containing sensitive records; account storage and uploaded recovery payloads remain encrypted with the account key.
